1The problem you already know

Fresh stock is a race against the clock.

Every morning you put out stock that has to sell before it spoils. Some of it will not. When it does not sell, you do not just lose the sale, you have already paid for the product, so it costs you twice.

The usual fix is a markdown sticker. But the big questions are guesswork: which items, how much off, and how early in the day. Mark down too soon or too deep and you give away margin you did not need to. Leave it too late and it goes in the bin anyway.

Offcut takes that one decision and gets it right, item by item, every day.

2What Offcut actually is

A markdown advisor that runs in the background.

Think of it as an extra set of eyes that watches your fresh stock all day and works out the smartest price for anything at risk of not selling. It does not change your prices on its own. It hands your team a simple list: this item, this much off, now. Your staff stay in control and decide whether to act.

Its single goal is the number that actually matters to you: the most profit left in your till after waste is counted. Not the most sales, not the least waste on its own, but the best balance of the two.

3How it works, step by step

From shelf to sticker in five plain steps.

It reads your stock

It looks at what is on the shelf today, how much there is, when each batch was made, and when it expires.

It works out the likely demand

Using your own sales history, it estimates how much of each item should sell today, allowing for the day of the week and the time of day. Saturdays sell harder than Mondays, and mornings differ from late afternoon.

It tests every markdown

It quietly tries each price step, full price, 10% off, 20%, and so on, and estimates the profit after waste for each one. A bigger discount sells more but earns less per unit. It finds the sweet spot.

It recommends the winner

It puts the best option on a simple action list for your team: which items to reduce, by how much, and the few to keep an eye on.

It keeps watching as the day goes

As real sales come through your till, it updates its view. If something is moving slower than expected, it nudges the recommendation before it is too late.

4What it weighs up

The things a good shopkeeper considers, every time.

You weigh these up in your head already. Offcut does it for every item at once, with no items forgotten and no mood swings.

How much is left

More stock than the day can realistically clear means more risk, and a stronger case for acting early.

How fast it is selling today

If something has barely moved despite a busy morning, that is a warning sign it reads in real time.

How close to expiry

An item with hours left is treated very differently from one with two days of shelf life to go.

How shoppers react to price

A small discount shifts some products a lot and others barely at all. It learns the difference per category.

How much of the day is left

A markdown at 9am has all day to work. The same markdown at 5pm has almost no time, so the maths changes.

Your real cost and margin

It always knows what each item cost you, so it never trades away more margin than it needs to.

5The built-in common sense

Sensible limits so it never does something silly.

Offcut has firm rules that stop it from over-reacting. These are not optional extras, they are part of how it thinks.

  • ✓It will not fire-sale too early. Deep clearance discounts are only allowed when an item is genuinely old and genuinely overstocked, and only when a gentler discount clearly will not be enough.
  • ✓It avoids selling below cost. It will only ever go below cost on the final day, and only when binning the item would lose you even more. The rest of the time, your cost is a hard floor.
  • ✓It keeps older stock cheaper than fresh. The older batch of the same product is never priced above a fresher one, so customers are never asked to pay more for less fresh.
  • ✓It will not chase pointless discounts. A deeper markdown has to clearly beat a smaller one before it is recommended. If a small reduction does the job, that is what you get.
  • ✓It holds its nerve on fresh stock. Items with days of shelf life left are left at full price unless there is a real overstock and a real gain from acting.

8The fine print, in plain words

What we need, and where it falls short.

What we need from you

  • Access to your till or point-of-sale sales data, so it can learn your shop.
  • A rough record of what spoils, so it can learn from real waste, not guesses.
  • Staff who can action the markdown list reasonably promptly. The advice goes stale if it sits for hours.
  • A bit of patience during shadow mode while it tunes to your shop.

Where it is still limited

  • It is proven in simulation, not yet across many live shops. You would be an early partner.
  • A brand-new product category needs a settling-in period before it advises on it.
  • It works best when markdowns are actioned on time. Long delays blunt the benefit.
  • It does not yet handle food-safety or staffing rules, so your team's judgement always comes first.
